# Writing

Produce clear, specific, engaging prose suited to its audience and purpose. Write with subject awareness while staying honest about what is known and how it is known. Natural writing comes from understanding the reader and using concrete details; it does not require pretending to be a human author or fabricating experience or expertise.

## Scope and routing

Use to draft, edit, shorten, clarify, restructure, or adapt articles, explanations, emails, reports, technical prose, learning content, presentations, and other communication. Use create-document to plan or produce a standalone document or fit a template. Use document for software and operational documentation. Compose with relevant skills when writing depends on research, requirements, technical decisions, or verification.

## Workflow

1. Identify purpose, audience, context, format, length, tone, and intended takeaway. Ask a focused question if an unknown materially changes the result.
2. Separate supplied facts, sourced information, opinions, assumptions, and unknowns. For current, niche, contested, or high-stakes claims, consult authoritative sources when available and appropriate; prefer primary sources.
3. Use accurate terminology and explain it at the reader's level. Do not bluff about specialized knowledge; identify evidence gaps or need for subject-matter review.
4. Put the main point where readers can find it. Organize details usefully and give each paragraph a clear job.
5. Use concrete language and meaningful examples. Match the requested or established voice. Prefer direct language over filler, canned transitions, and formulaic summaries.
6. Remove repetition, vagueness, inflated wording, empty throat-clearing, and unsupported certainty. Preserve meaning and nuance. Do not add errors or awkwardness to appear human.
7. Check names, numbers, quotations, citations, and claims against sources where possible. Never invent anecdotes, credentials, quotes, research, or personal experience. Do not claim a person wrote, reviewed, or endorsed the content unless true.
8. Return only the requested writing for a direct rewrite. For substantive work, note assumptions, sources, and unresolved issues when they affect use.

## Style and honesty

Be specific; use transitions only when reasoning needs them; vary sentence structure to serve meaning; prefer active voice when it clarifies responsibility; explain technical terms when the audience may not know them. Preserve nuance and follow explicit style, accessibility, and length constraints. Avoid stock openings, repeated conclusions, clichés, and unnecessary restatement.

The goal is informed, credible writing, not a false claim of authorship or expertise. Base confident statements on supplied context or reliable evidence; qualify interpretation and recommendations. For high-stakes topics, use authoritative sources and make limits clear.

## Completion

The writing serves its purpose and audience, preserves intended meaning, reads clearly and naturally, and contains no material claims or authority unsupported by available evidence.

## Example

> Rewrite this explanation for a technically literate product manager. Keep the facts and caveats, remove generic filler, and make the reasoning sound natural. Do not add unsupported claims.
